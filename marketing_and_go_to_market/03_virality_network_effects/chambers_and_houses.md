# Chambers & Houses — the Network-Effect Engine & Community Moat

Solo players churn; factions compound. **Chambers** (competitive teams) and **Houses** (invite-only crews) are the two social structures that convert individual Operators into a self-reinforcing network — and into the moat. The mechanics rhyme but the jobs differ: **Houses are the recruiting engine (K-factor); Chambers are the retention engine (team obligation).** Together they create the network effect where *every Operator makes the network more valuable to the next.* Reference the community playbooks in `../04_community/`.

> Reputation here is **non-portable.** A whale can buy chips on any platform; they cannot buy a Council seat, a Chamber legend, or a House they founded. That non-portability is the defensibility — the reason an Operator can't just leave (`../00_foundation/lexicon_and_status_system.md`).

## House vs. Chamber — two structures, two jobs

| | **House** | **Chamber** |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| What | invite-only crew/room an Operator **founds** | competitive **team/guild** an Operator **joins** |
| Primary job | **virality / K-factor** (recruit to staff & grow) | **retention** (team obligation + collective standing) |
| Texture | private, exclusive, founder-owned | public-facing, competitive, season-long |
| Economics | founder **points / rev share** | collective board rewards, Chamber multipliers |
| Growth loop | #2 House referral (`viral_loops_and_referrals.md`) | #4 Chamber/team |
| Shareable | "Join my House" Blink card | Chamber crest + standing card |

An Operator typically lives in **both**: founds/joins a House (their personal faction) *and* plays for a Chamber (their team). The two loops interlock — Houses feed members into the ecosystem; Chambers keep them there.

## Houses: founding, joining & economics

**Founding flow.** Any Operator can found a House → name + crest → gets a private House page, private tables, an internal leaderboard, and an optional **rake/points share** on House activity. Founding is a designed progression event (`../02_activation_retention/progression_and_ranks.md`).

**Joining flow.** By **Blink-invite** (one-tap "join my House" Action in a tweet → wallet connect → seated in *that* House, same session — see `viral_loops_and_referrals.md`) or by founder admission. Invite-only by design.

**House economics — why founders recruit relentlessly:**

- A founder earns a **share of their House's match activity** (points now; rev/points multiplier with the token meta — `../05_economy_incentives/incentive_and_anti_sybil_design.md`). More active Operators → bigger share → direct, recurring incentive to recruit *and* retain.
- A founder's **contribution rating** rises with a thriving House (feeds the climb), and House insignia confer **status** — the founder is *seen* as the head of a strong faction.
- Crucially the reward is **recurring and play-based**, not a one-shot bounty — so the founder's incentive is to build a durable, active crew, not to spam links. (Quality-weighted, anti-sybil; empty wallets pay nothing — `viral_loops_and_referrals.md`.)

This is what makes the House a K-engine instead of a coupon: the founder *wants* members because the House is theirs and pays them in status and upside.

## Chambers: founding, joining & competition

**Founding/joining flow.** Operators band into a Chamber (or charter a new one) → shared crest, collective leaderboard, Chamber rituals, season standings. Joining is a progression event timed *after* the first Houses, so the Operator is already hooked before we add team obligation (`../02_activation_retention/onboarding_and_aha_moment.md`).

**Chamber-vs-Chamber events (the team spectacle):**

- **Chamber Night** (weekly ritual, `../02_activation_retention/retention_engine.md`): Chambers field Operators; results move the **Chamber season board**. "We're one Operator short tonight" is the highest-converting retention push there is — pure social obligation.
- **Chamber wars / grudge matches:** scheduled head-to-heads between rival Chambers → spectatable, clip-worthy (`spectator_and_clip_engine.md`), and a recruiting magnet (winning Chambers draft the strongest free Operators).
- **Collective standing** means an individual's churn *costs their teammates* — that's the retention mechanism. You don't quit on your Chamber mid-season.

Chambers are the **ambassador program in disguise** — competing Chambers recruit and evangelize on our behalf to win (`../04_community/ambassador_and_chamber_program.md`).

## The retention × recruiting duality

The system's power is that the two motions reinforce instead of trading off:

```
HOUSE  (recruiting / K)                 CHAMBER  (retention)
founder needs Operators  ───────────►   recruited Operators join Chambers
to fill tables & grow share             collective standing creates obligation
        ▲                                          │
        │  new Operators found their own Houses     │ obligation → daily play
        └──────────  more recruiting  ◄─────────────┘ retained base to recruit from
```

- **Houses bring people in** (selfish, status-bearing invites → K-factor).
- **Chambers keep people in** (team obligation, collective standing → retention).
- A **retained base** is exactly what recruiting needs to compound (you recruit *from* and *into* a sticky network), and a **growing base** gives Chambers fresh talent to draft. Virality × retention, not virality vs. retention — the two terms of `Operators(t+1) = Operators(t) × (Retention × K)` (`../00_foundation/growth_model_and_flywheel.md`).

## Network effects (why each Operator makes Syndicate better)

- **Liquidity:** more Operators in Houses/Chambers → fuller tables → less cold-start, better matches → more reason to join. Direct same-side network effect on a multiplayer game.
- **Status legibility:** rank only means something against a populated field; every new Operator makes the Council/Chamber boards *more* prestigious to be on — so existing Operators recruit to inflate the value of their own standing.
- **Content supply:** more factions → more rivalries, grudge matches, and clips (`spectator_and_clip_engine.md`) → more acquisition → more factions.
- **Reputation compounding:** the longer a Chamber competes / a House thrives, the more lore and legend accrue to it — value that *cannot be exported*.

## The community moat (non-portable reputation)

This is the deepest defensibility Syndicate has, and it lives here:

1. **Reputation is wallet-bound and Syndicate-specific.** Your Council appearances, Grand Operator titles, Chamber legends, and founded Houses exist *only here* and persist across season resets (`../02_activation_retention/progression_and_ranks.md`). A competitor can copy mechanics; they cannot copy your history.
2. **Switching cost is social, not financial.** Leaving means abandoning a Chamber that depends on you and a House you built — abandoning *people and standing*, not just a balance. Far stickier than any deposit lock.
3. **Founders are anchored operators.** A House founder has built equity (members, share, status) they won't walk away from — they become our most retained, most evangelizing segment.
4. **The network defends itself.** Chambers recruit to win, founders recruit to grow, the Council aspires to defend — the community does acquisition and retention as a *byproduct of competing for status*. That is a moat that widens as it scales.

A clone can ship the sealed-reveal mechanic in a quarter. It cannot ship five seasons of Council storylines, a thousand founded Houses, and the Chamber rivalries that make this *the* arena. Mechanics are copyable; a status civilization is not.

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|--------|------------|--------|
| **House-attach** | activated Operators in ≥1 House | **≥ 40%** |
| **Chamber-attach** | activated Operators in a Chamber | **≥ 35%** |
| **Founder rate** | Operators who found a House | **≥ 25%** |
| **Chamber retention lift** | W4 of Chamber members vs. non | **≥ +15pp** (`../02_activation_retention/retention_engine.md`) |
