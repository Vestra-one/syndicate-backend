# KOL & Creator Program — KOL-as-Operator

This program is the leverage tier of the portfolio (`channel_strategy.md`) and the engine behind the **KOL-as-Operator Loop (6)**: a KOL gets a House + a Council fast-*lane* + upside → their status compounds when their audience plays → they recruit their audience → the audience produces clips → more KOLs want in (`../00_foundation/growth_model_and_flywheel.md`).

## The core inversion: we don't buy posts, we mint Operators

The default Solana KOL motion is **pay-per-post**: a flat fee, one promoted tweet, one terminal impression, zero durable asset, and a mercenary incentive that evaporates the day the check clears. **We reject it entirely** (it's the banned channel in `channel_strategy.md`).

Instead, a KOL becomes an **Operator** with:
- a **House** they own and recruit into,
- **House points / revenue share** on the activity they drive,
- a **featured Operator profile** and **early access**,
- a **fast-lane to consideration** — not a free rank.

The result: a flat fee buys one tweet; **House upside buys a recruiter who works for years because their own status and stake now depend on the table being full.** Their CAC amortizes across every Operator their House ever seats.

## The non-negotiable: status is EARNED, never bought

This is the line that protects the entire skill brand (`../00_foundation/positioning_and_brand.md`). We will be explicit, internally and in every KOL contract:

> **A KOL can be *given* a House, a profile, a revenue share, and early access. A KOL cannot be *given* a rank, a Council seat, or the Grand Operator title. Those are earned at the table, on-chain, like everyone else.**

Why we hold this line:
- The moment rank is purchasable, The Council is worthless, and the Status Loop (3) — our highest-margin acquisition — collapses.
- The Sharp Trader and the competitor persona we want will *smell* a bought leaderboard instantly and leave.
- "Fast-lane" means **early access and visibility**, i.e., the KOL gets to the table sooner and is *seen* sooner. What happens at the table is theirs to earn.

So a Founding KOL who plays badly sits low on the leaderboard publicly — and that is *fine*, even good: it is proof the game is real. We sell them distribution and upside, not a trophy.

## Tiers

| Tier | Who | Slots | Term |
|------|-----|-------|------|
| **Founding KOL** | Top trading/poker/Solana voices; launch-defining names | ~10–15, Genesis only | 12 mo + renewal |
| **Featured Operator** | Mid-tier creators, strong streamers, sharp niche voices | ~50 | 6 mo rolling |
| **Affiliate** | The long tail; any Operator who recruits | Open / permissionless | Evergreen |

| Benefit | Founding KOL | Featured Operator | Affiliate |
|---------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Owned **House** | Yes — named, featured | Yes | Yes (self-serve) |
| **House points / rev share** | Top rate | Standard rate | Base rate |
| **Featured profile** (site + CT) | Yes, hero placement | Yes | — |
| **Early access** (pre-public waves) | Genesis Council wave | Whitelist wave | Public |
| **Council fast-lane** (early seat at the table — *not* a rank) | Yes | Partial | — |

| **Earned** rank / Council seat / Grand Operator | Like everyone | Like everyone | Like everyone |

Affiliate is intentionally permissionless: it *is* the House referral loop (2) made available to all, so the program has no floor — anyone can start recruiting via a Blink (`crypto_twitter_playbook.md`).

## Deliverables (what we ask for)

Deliverables are framed as **Operator behavior**, not "ad units" — we want them to *play and post as themselves*, because authenticity is the asset.

- **Founding KOL:** play ≥N contested matches/month; ≥2 reveal-clip posts/week (their own hands, via the Clip Engine); host ≥1 House recruiting push/month; participate in ≥1 exhibition or co-stream/quarter; appear in the Genesis narrative (`launch_campaign.md`).
- **Featured Operator:** play regularly; ≥1 reveal post/week; keep their House active.
- **Affiliate:** recruit via their House Blink. No content minimum — pure performance.

Note the absence of "say these talking points." We hand them the Clip Engine and the lexicon; the product produces the content. A scripted KOL post reads as an ad and devalues the brand.

## Anti-mercenary alignment

Mercenary capital is real on Solana and we don't moralize about it — we **align it** (this is the same logic as the Speculation Loop, `../05_economy_incentives/`). The structure does the work:

- **Upside > flat fees.** Compensation skews to House rev/points share, which only pays if the KOL's audience actually plays and *retains*. A KOL who dumps a low-quality audience earns little.
- **Vesting / cliff on token-path allocations.** Allocations tied to sustained activity, not a one-time post — no farm-and-dump.
- **Performance-gated promotion.** Featured Operator → more visibility is earned by House activity and clip performance, not negotiated.
- **Skin in the game.** Founding KOLs play with real buy-ins like anyone; they're Operators, not mascots. Their own pot is on the table.
- **No rank-selling, ever** (see above) — which also means there's nothing to "dump." You can't sell a status you can only earn.

The filter is self-enforcing: a KOL motivated only by a flat check won't like a deal that pays on retention — and that's precisely the KOL we don't want.

## Sourcing

Three pools, mapped to the persona graph (`../00_foundation/target_segments_personas.md`):

1. **Trading CT** — perps and DeFi voices whose audiences already take risk and respect a fair game (the Drift / Jupiter / marginfi / Kamino / Zeta crowd). Highest-fit: they convert into the Sharp Trader persona. *(Their platforms are also partner targets — see `ecosystem_partnerships.md`.)*
2. **Poker CT / poker creators** — the *exact* mental model of Syndicate is poker (sealed conviction bet like a no-limit hand, side-pot rules). Poker streamers and theorists translate our skill story natively and lend instant credibility to the "financial sport" frame.
3. **Solana streamers / content creators** — the spectacle and clip producers. They feed the Clip Loop directly and bring the spectator audience that the co-stream program converts.

Outreach posture: **invitation, not solicitation.** We approach as "a seat is open for you," consistent with the invite-textured brand. The DM is terse, premium, and leads with the House + upside, never with a fee.

## Streamer / co-stream program

Live spectacle is the bridge from spectator to Operator (Clip Loop + Status Loop):

- **Co-streamed exhibitions.** We seat a Founding KOL and a poker creator at a high-stakes table and run it as a broadcast (the launch exhibition is the template — `launch_campaign.md`). The stream produces a VOD and a stack of reveal clips that outlive it.
- **Streamer House nights.** A streamer runs a live session recruiting their chat into *their* House via an on-stream Blink overlay — discovery → seat in one tap, on air.
- **Clip rights + kit.** Co-streamers get the Clip Engine integration and overlay assets so every dramatic reveal is instantly a luxury clip they (and we) reshare.
- **Coronation co-streams.** The first Grand Operator coronation is co-streamed across multiple creator channels simultaneously — one ceremony, many audiences, one shared status moment.

Selection for the marquee slots is **performance- and fit-gated**, not pay-gated — keeping faith with the earned-status rule even in the spotlight.

## How this program ignites the launch

In the launch sequence (`launch_campaign.md`), the KOL program lights **first**, alongside the Clip Loop: Founding KOLs are the **Genesis Council** wave, their Houses become the initial recruiting graph, and their exhibition co-streams produce the first clips that pull in the public. KOLs aren't a growth tactic bolted on later — they are how the flywheel gets its first push from credible hands.
